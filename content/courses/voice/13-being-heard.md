A light voice can feel easy to lose in a busy room. Being heard is mostly about where your sound goes and how clearly you shape it, not how hard you push. If your throat tightens, tickles or tires, stop and rest: pushing over noise is one of the quickest ways to strain a voice.

**Get closer first.** Stepping nearer, facing the person and waiting for a gap in the noise do more than any extra volume. Let the room help you.

**Aim it.** Pick a spot just past the person's ear and send the words there, as if passing them a note across the table. Your sound tends to follow your attention.

**Open up.** Let your mouth open a little more on the vowels and keep the consonants crisp. Clear edges carry further than extra loudness, and they keep the voice light.

**Ride the breath.** Take an easy breath before the line and let the words go out on it. If you feel yourself pushing from the throat, the voice turns heavier: go back to the light "uh-oh" from [Vocal weight](#/studio/weight) and try again.

**Short and paced.** Short phrases with small pauses are easier to catch in noise than one long run.

**Listen back.** Record from a little further away than usual, about an arm's length. Does it still sound light, and could you keep it up for a whole conversation? If it sounds pushed, take it back a step.

## say-again

Sorry, could you say that again? It's a bit loud in here.

> Your check-in. Picture the person close by, aim past their ear and keep it light: clearer, not louder.

## spell-name

It's for Sam. S, A, M.

> Crisp consonants carry. Spell it with a small pause between letters, as if passing a note. Use your own name, if you'd rather.

## over-here

Hey, over here! I saved you a seat.

> Calling across a room. Open the "hey" and "here" and aim past your friend. If your throat tightens, wave instead and try again more quietly.

## the-bill

Shall we get the bill? I can pay for mine by card.

> A busy table. Two short phrases with a breath between them do the work.

## one-thing

Can I just add one thing before we move on?

> Joining in. Wait for a gap, then start clearly from the first word rather than building up to it.

## at-the-back

Can everyone at the back hear me all right?

> Record this one from an arm's length away. Still light, just aimed further.
