Vocal weight is how heavy or light your voice sounds: a booming "HEY!" across a field is heavy, a soft "uh-oh" to a toddler is light. It's partly separate from pitch and loudness: they move together, but you can learn to steer weight on its own. Lighter, but still clear, is where many people want to go, if it's what you want.

**Feel the difference.** At the same pitch and an ordinary loudness, say a sturdy "HEY!" like greeting a friend across a room, then a soft "awww" like you've just seen a puppy. The second uses thinner, lighter vibration. That's the light end, and you get it without adding air.

**Go light, not breathy.** Breathiness is a different thing: it covers a heavy voice up rather than lightening it, and it wastes air and tires and dries your voice. Don't go quiet or whispery to sound softer either; speak at your normal level. If you hear a lot of air, add a touch more clarity. If you feel a push in your chest or throat, back off.

**Borrow a light moment.** Little sounds are naturally light: "uh-oh", "huh?", a gentle "aww". Start from one of them and carry its feel into the next words.

**Use it in words.** "Hello, how are you today?" with the same light, clear feel, at an ordinary speaking volume.

**Listen back.** Is it lighter than your everyday take, and still easy to understand? Lilt doesn't measure weight, so this one is all your ears. Playing two takes back to back helps a lot.

## light-hello

aaah… hello.

> Your check-in: a light, clear "ah" that flows into "hello". Compare with an earlier take.

## middle

aaah · aaah · aaah

> Three ahs on one easy note: sturdy like "HEY", then soft like a cooing "aww", then that soft one again with no extra air. Play it back; is the last one light and still clear?

## uh-oh

uh-oh! · huh? · aww

> Say them the way you'd say them to a small child: light and easy. Notice how little effort they take.

## whimper

nyeh · nyeh · nyeh

> A small, playful whimper, like a puppy asking for a treat, with a little downward slide. Relaxed and fairly quiet, never breathy.

## light-phrase

Hello, how are you today?

> Bring the light, clear feel into the sentence. If it turns breathy, add a little more voice; if it gets heavy, think "uh-oh".

## light-count

One, two, three, four, five.

> Keep every number as light as the first, and let the pitch move naturally.
