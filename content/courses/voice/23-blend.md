Your voice has several dials, and they turn separately. Pitch goes higher or lower. Resonance, the size of the space your voice sounds in, goes brighter or darker. Weight, how much of your vocal folds meets as they vibrate, goes lighter or fuller. Melody is how much your voice moves and how you lean on words. Listeners put all of them together, so an in-between voice doesn't have to sit in the middle of every dial: it can be a mix. A middle pitch with a brighter space, a lower pitch with a lighter weight, or a higher pitch with a darker, fuller sound can all land somewhere in between.

**Know your dials.** Pick one and play with it while the others stay put. Most voices move pitch and weight together, thinner up high and thicker down low, so keeping them apart takes a little practice. That's what this chapter is for.

**Move the space, keep the note.** Hum on one easy note and chew slowly, as if on a toffee. Then add a small smile, and then the start of a yawn. The pitch stays the same; the sound gets brighter with the smile and darker with the yawn. While you record in this chapter, a darker–brighter strip under the pitch line shows which way the sound moved since the take began.

**Hear bright and dark at your middle.** From your middle, pant like a big dog, slow and deep: "hhuh, hhuh". Then like a small dog, quick and light: "hhih, hhih". Somewhere between the two is a space you can settle in, or lean one way on purpose.

**Hear light and full at one note.** Say "uh-oh" the way you would to a toddler, then a friendly "hey!" to someone across the street, at about the same note (a call, not a shout). The first is lighter, the second fuller. Try a word halfway between.

**Try a recipe.** Choose two dials and one setting for each, such as "middle pitch, a little brighter" or "a bit lower, a bit lighter". Record the same line with two recipes, then open **Detailed comparison** under the newer take to hear them side by side. Keep the one you like and give it a name of your own.

**Melody your way.** To lean on a word you can go up in pitch, get a little louder, make the word longer or pause before it. Big jumps up tend to be heard as more feminine, and loudness and pauses as less, but expression is personal. Mixing them is fine, and so is a voice that moves a lot.

**Your ears first.** The pitch line and the strip are rough clues, and a different mic or distance moves them. Play two takes back to back and ask which one sounds more like you, not which one hits a number.

**Keep it gentle.** Each dial should turn without effort. Brighter is never squeezed, darker is never pushed down, lighter is never breathy and fuller is never pressed or shouted. If your throat tightens or your voice tires, let the dials go back to your everyday voice, shake out your jaw and rest.

## glad-you-came

Hi, I'm really glad you came. Come in, the kettle's just boiled.

> Your check-in: read it with the mix you like best today. Same words every visit, so you can hear how your mix changes.

## chew-hum

mmm (chew) … mmm (smile) … mmm (yawn)

> Hum on one easy note the whole time. Chew, then smile, then start a yawn, and listen to the colour change while the note stays put.

## big-small-dog

hhuh, hhuh · hhih, hhih

> Big dog, then small dog, from your middle and on an easy breath. Then find a pant halfway between.

## light-full

uh-oh · hey!

> A toddler "uh-oh", then a friendly "hey!" across the street, at about the same note and an ordinary volume. Never shout it.

## recipe-one

Good morning. Did you sleep all right?

> Say it with one recipe, such as your middle pitch and a slightly brighter space. Then again with another, such as a little lower and a little lighter. Compare them.

## same-note

How was your weekend? Mine was quiet.

> Keep the pitch roughly the same and change only the weight: once lighter, once fuller. Notice how differently it comes across.

## lean-mix

I didn't say Tuesday. I said Thursday.

> Lean on "Tuesday" and "Thursday" four ways: higher, louder, longer, or with a pause before. Which ones feel like you?

## short-story

We missed the last bus, so we walked home along the canal, talking about nothing much, and it turned out to be the best part of the night.

> A longer line in your mix. If one dial drifts back as you go, that's normal: take a breath and set it again.
