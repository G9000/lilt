Longer lines give you words to lean on while you practise one familiar quality.

**Listen first.** Replay a short phrase you liked. Choose one cue to bring into the reading; the passage is not a test of every skill at once.

**Try one thing.** Start with one sentence, then add another if it stays easy. Pause and breathe where the meaning and your comfort call for it; punctuation is a guide, not a breathing timer.

**Let sentences finish.** A natural fall in pitch is fine. Listen for whether the quality you wanted is still there, rather than lifting every last word.

**Use it in your own words.** After a sentence or paragraph, say one line about what happened in it. That small change from reading to speaking is part of today's practice.

**Listen back.** Compare the start and end for comfort and the quality you chose, allowing normal melody. Shorten the passage if attention or effort becomes difficult.

**Ready for more?** A short passage and a sentence of your own feel manageable. There is no need to finish a long text to make the visit count.

## market

Every Saturday morning, I walk down to the little market by the river. The air smells of warm bread and fresh lemons, and everyone seems to be in a good mood. I usually buy honey from the same woman, who always remembers my name. Then I find a bench in the sun, open my notebook, and let the morning move slowly around me.

> Your check-in: start with a sentence or read more if comfortable. Pause naturally; afterwards, tell yourself one thing about the market without reading.

## s1

My mother made lemon meringue on Monday morning.

> Try an easy m into the words, then say the sentence normally. Listen for the quality you wanted without holding a buzz throughout.

## s2

We were walking along the river in the evening.

> Read as an ordinary sentence with natural emphasis and breathing. Keep just one practice cue in mind.

## s4

I'm meeting Lena near the main library at noon.

> Choose where the meaning needs emphasis. Your pitch can rise and fall naturally.

## rain

The rain started just after lunch, light at first and then heavier. I made a cup of tea, wrapped myself in a blanket, and listened to it drumming on the window. There was nowhere I needed to be. Sometimes the best afternoons are the ones where nothing happens at all, and the world feels a little quieter than usual.

> Listen for your chosen quality near the start and end. A falling ending is fine; shorten the passage if it becomes effortful.

## garden

My neighbour has a tiny garden full of mint, lavender and tall yellow flowers. In the evening, when the light turns golden, she waters everything by hand and hums to herself. I once asked her what the song was. She laughed and said she had no idea; she just made it up as she went along, a little different every night.

> Read a comfortable amount, then summarise one detail in your own words. Reset with a pause if useful; do not force the pitch back up.
