Resonance is the space your voice travels through after it leaves your vocal folds: your throat and mouth. A shorter, smaller space sounds brighter and a longer, bigger one darker, the way a short pipe rings higher than a long one. For listeners it matters about as much as pitch, and in some studies more.

**Hear the difference first.** Softly whisper "ee" and slide it up and down like a siren, just for a few seconds (no loud stage whisper). You'll hear the whisper itself get brighter and darker, with no voice at all. That change is resonance. Play the chapter video for a voiced demonstration.

**Make the space smaller.** Three things do it. Your tongue: forward, not pulled back into your throat. Your lips: a slight smile instead of rounded. Your larynx (voice box): it can float up a little, which shortens the space.

**Find your larynx.** Rest a fingertip lightly on your throat and swallow: you'll feel the larynx rise and drop. Now pant a few times like a tiny dog, then like a big one. The tiny dog keeps it up. Then say "wa wa wa" with the tiny-dog feeling, and keep that lift into words. A few pants is plenty; stop if you feel dizzy.

**Brightness isn't pitch.** Brightening tends to drag your pitch up with it. Practise moving them separately: hold one note and make only the brightness change, then keep the brightness and let the pitch drop a little. The pitch line should stay put while the sound changes.

**Try it in sound.** Hum, then open into a bright "ee" with a hint of a smile. Then the words. Small changes count; you're not holding a pose.

**Use it in words.** "Morning! How are you?" after an easy hum. Is the brightness still there once the words start?

**Listen back fairly.** Compare like with like: the same vowel, a similar pitch and loudness, the same mic distance. Lilt compares each held vowel with your first take of it. It's a rough reading and tends to understate real change, so trust your ears first.

**If it feels tight,** you're squeezing rather than shrinking. Reset with an easy hum or the straw from the warm-ups, then try a smaller change.

## ee

eeeeee

> Your check-in: an easy "ee" as in "see", about four seconds. Keep your pitch and loudness close to last time so the comparison is fair.

## bright-morning

Morning! How are you?

> Hum, then say it with the small brighter "ee" space you found. Compare with an ordinary take.

## whisper-siren

eee ↗ ↘ · eee

> Softly whisper the sliding "ee" first and listen to the whisper brighten as it goes up. Then say the last "ee" out loud, keeping the bright version of that space.

## small-dog

wa · wa · wa

> Pant like a tiny dog a few times, then say "wa" with that lifted feeling. Listen for a brighter "wa" than your usual one.

## bright-slide

eee · eee · eee

> One easy note: a darker "ee", a brighter "ee", then darker again. Only the brightness moves; check the pitch line stays flat.

## forward-hum

mmmmmmm

> Hum with a small smile and feel for a buzz on your lips or the front of your face. If you don't feel one, that's fine; listen instead.

## m-vowels

mmmee · mmmaa · mmmoo

> Open the hum into each vowel. "Oo" will always be darker than "ee"; try to keep the tongue forward even there.

## m-words

many · maybe · mellow · morning · lemon

> Lean into each m for a moment, then release into the word. The m sets the space; the word keeps it.

## giggle

eye · air · ear

> Do a small, silent giggle first and notice the little upward lift in your throat, and the smaller space behind your tongue. Then say each word with that feeling. Skip it if it makes you tense.

## ah

aaaaaa

> An easy "ah" as in "father", about four seconds. "Ah" is naturally darker than "ee", so only compare it with your earlier "ah".

## oo

oooooo

> An easy "oo" as in "food". Lips round for "oo", but let the tongue stay a little forward. Compare only with your earlier "oo".
