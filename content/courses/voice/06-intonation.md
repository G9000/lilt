Intonation is the melody and emphasis of speech. You get to choose how expressive, gentle or matter-of-fact you want to sound.

**Listen first.** Listen to an ordinary greeting in a conversation or a recording you like. Notice which word matters and how the phrase ends. A falling ending can be completely natural.

**Try one thing.** Say a line with two different intentions: curious, reassuring or telling a story. Change one emphasis and listen to whether the meaning comes across. Exaggeration can help you explore, but you can return to an ordinary conversational version straight away.

**Use it in words.** Try "Hi, good morning. How are you?" as if someone you know is there. Let the meaning guide the rises, falls and pauses; every question does not need an upward ending.

**Listen back.** Does it sound like the intention you chose, and does it feel like you? A wavier pitch line is not automatically better. You can be feminine, expressive or warm with more than one melody.

**Ready for more?** You can repeat an emphasis you like and use it comfortably in a short real phrase.

## melody

It was the first warm morning of spring, and the whole street seemed to wake up at once.

> Your check-in: read it with the meaning you want to convey. Listen to whether the emphasis and pauses sound natural to you; there is no required shape for the pitch line.

## everyday-meaning

Hi, good morning. How are you?

> Try a curious version and a relaxed familiar version. Listen for the intention; choose one you like without forcing every ending upward.

## storyteller

Once upon a time, a tiny cat found a door that had never been opened before.

> Try a storyteller version, then an ordinary version. Keep one expressive change you like, with comfortable effort.

## gentle-news

I'm so sorry, the café is closed today, but they're open again tomorrow.

> Say it as a little piece of disappointing news. Compare two gentle ways of saying it and decide which feels most like you.

## question

Oh, really? Are you coming too?

> Try genuine curiosity, then a quieter check that you heard correctly. Listen for the difference; a rise at every question ending is not required.

## emphasis

I didn't say she took it. I said she borrowed it.

> Make the contrast between say and borrowed easy to hear. Try changing timing, emphasis or pitch without forcing a bigger range.

## exaggerate

Could I get a coffee, please? Thank you so much!

> Try an expressive version if comfortable, then a conversational one. Keep the part you like; you do not need to keep the exaggeration.
