Reading aloud gives you the words, so you can keep your voice going for longer than a phrase. It's the bridge between short lines and talking freely.

**Start small.** One sentence, then two, then the paragraph. Stop at the length where your voice still feels like your practice voice.

**Breathe with the meaning.** Pause at commas and full stops, and anywhere you'd naturally take a breath telling someone this. Each breath is a free moment to reset.

**Let sentences land.** Pitch naturally falls at the end of a sentence. That's fine; just make sure the next sentence starts back at your note rather than staying down.

**Use it in your own words.** After reading, tell the room one thing about the passage without looking at it. That small switch from reading to talking is the most useful part.

**Listen back.** Compare the first sentence with the last. If the end has drifted, read a shorter piece next time and reset at every full stop.

## market

Every Saturday morning, I walk down to the little market by the river. The air smells of warm bread and fresh lemons, and everyone seems to be in a good mood. I usually buy honey from the same woman, who always remembers my name. Then I find a bench in the sun, open my notebook, and let the morning move slowly around me.

> Your check-in: read as much as feels good, then say one thing about the market without looking.

## s1

My mother made lemon meringue on Monday morning.

> Full of m's: let each one set up the bright space, then keep it through the sentence.

## s2

We were walking along the river in the evening.

> An ordinary sentence with ordinary emphasis. Keep just one cue in mind.

## s4

I'm meeting Lena near the main library at noon.

> Pick the word that matters most (Lena? noon?) and lean on it a little.

## rain

The rain started just after lunch, light at first and then heavier. I made a cup of tea, wrapped myself in a blanket, and listened to it drumming on the window. There was nowhere I needed to be. Sometimes the best afternoons are the ones where nothing happens at all, and the world feels a little quieter than usual.

> A calm one. Reset at each full stop, and notice whether the last sentence still sounds like the first.

## garden

My neighbour has a tiny garden full of mint, lavender and tall yellow flowers. In the evening, when the light turns golden, she waters everything by hand and hums to herself. I once asked her what the song was. She laughed and said she had no idea; she just made it up as she went along, a little different every night.

> Read a bit, then tell it back in your own words. Did your voice change when you stopped reading?

## shadow-read

The kettle clicked off, and the kitchen filled with warm steam.

> Record it, then play it back while mouthing along, and read it again straight after. Copy your own rhythm and melody, then keep them when you read on.
