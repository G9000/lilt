## Current status

There are no dedicated A/B clips in this folder yet. The course uses its linked demonstration
videos and the learner's own saved takes. Do not add placeholder audio links, copy a creator's
video audio, or present generated speech or tones as a demonstration of human voice technique.

## Brief for the first human examples

Record one speaker demonstrating two comfortable possibilities, A and B, with the same words,
mic and distance. Explain which quality is being varied and what also changed. These are listening
contrasts, not a correct/incorrect pair or a model everyone must match. Allow ordinary pitch movement
in spoken examples. A voice professional should review the teaching claims before these are offered
as technique demonstrations.

| Exercise | Short recording brief | Listening question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm-up `hum` | One easy hum opening into “Hello, how are you?” | Does the greeting begin as easily as the hum? |
| Pitch `easy-hello` | Two comfortable starting pitches on “Hello, how are you?” | Can you hear the pitch difference while recognising the other differences too? |
| Resonance `ee` | Two easy “ee” vowels with a brighter/darker contrast, then a greeting; similar pitch and loudness | What changed in the vowel quality, and can you hear it in speech? |
| Weight `light-phrase` | Two clear versions of “Hello, how are you today?” with a small weight contrast; similar pitch and loudness | Can you hear the intended contrast without treating quietness or breathiness as the target? |
| Intonation `everyday-meaning` | “Hi, good morning. How are you?” with two intentions | Which emphasis or melody makes each intention audible? |

Keep clips short and label the speaker and intended contrast. Record yourself or a consenting
speaker; document permission to include the file and its attribution. Never label somebody else's
natural voice as a failure example. Only attach a real file with `example:` after checking that it
plays and that its teaching note accurately describes it.
