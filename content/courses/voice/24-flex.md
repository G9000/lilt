Some people want one voice that stays put. Others want to move: a little lighter with some people, a little fuller with others, depending on where they are, who's listening and how safe it feels. Both are real goals, and flexibility can be the main one. Everyone already does a bit of this, with a phone voice, a shop voice or a voice for talking to a cat. This chapter makes that movement something you choose.

**Start from home.** Your middle from [In between](#/studio/in-between), with the mix you liked in [Mixing the dials](#/studio/blend), is home base. You move out from it and come back to it. Keep it the voice that costs you least.

**Three versions of one line.** Say the same short line lighter, then from home, then fuller. Change only one or two dials at first, such as pitch and weight. Smaller moves are easier to make on demand than big ones.

**Slide, don't jump.** Count to ten, starting at one end and moving a little with each number until you reach the other, then come back. A slow slide teaches you where the in-between steps are.

**Switch mid-conversation.** In real life you might answer the phone in one voice and turn to a friend in another. Practise a line that changes partway, with a breath at the switch.

**Choose by situation.** Think about where you speak most: home, work or school, with friends, on the phone, in a noisy café. Pick one place and one voice for it, and practise a line you'd actually say there. The phone often needs the most practice, since your voice is all the listener has.

**Let your goals move too.** What you want may change as you hear more of what your voice can do. Every few weeks, listen to your check-in takes side by side and ask whether your home base still fits. Moving it is not starting over.

**Keep it gentle.** Moving between voices should never cost effort. If one end of your range only comes with a squeeze, a push or a tired throat by the evening, make the move smaller and spend more time at home base. Stop and rest if anything hurts or strains.

## three-ways

Hi, how are you doing?

> Your check-in: say it lighter, then from your home base, then fuller, on one take. Same words every visit, so you can hear your range of choices grow.

## slide-count

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten.

> Start at one end and move a little with each number until you reach the other end. Only as far as stays easy. Next time, slide back the other way.

## phone

Hi, I'm calling about the flat on Mill Road. Is it still available?

> A phone line. Choose a voice for it before you start, and keep it through to the question.

## friends

No way! Tell me everything. I want all the details.

> Among friends, where voices like to move. Let it be lively in whichever direction feels like you.

## work

Thanks, everyone. Shall we get started?

> A work or study voice. Choose how light or full you'd like it to be in that room, and keep it steady and unhurried.

## switch

Sorry, one second, I'm on the phone. Hi, yes, I'm still here.

> Two voices in one line: a side comment to someone in the room, then back to the call. Take a breath at the switch.

## home-again

I'm home. Do you want a cup of tea?

> Back to home base, the voice that costs you least. Notice how it feels after the others.
