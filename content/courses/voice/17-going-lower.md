Hi, friend. This part of the course is for a deeper, darker or fuller voice. It has its own four skill chapters, one dial each: [Pitch, going lower](#/studio/lower-pitch), [Resonance, going darker](#/studio/lower-resonance), [Vocal weight, going fuller](#/studio/lower-weight) and [Emphasis and melody](#/studio/lower-emphasis). With Which way set to Lower in [Practice settings](#/studio/settings), they're your Today's cards in place of the lighter skill chapters; open them from here whenever you like too. Warm-ups, and the texts in Bring it together and the Extras, work in any direction alongside them.

**Tell Lilt your direction.** Set **Which way** to Lower in [Practice settings](#/studio/settings). Lilt then only offers to move your range down, and never nudges you up.

**If you take testosterone.** Testosterone thickens the vocal folds, and that change is permanent: thicker folds can vibrate more slowly, so lower notes come within reach. It arrives at its own pace. Some people hear a difference within weeks, others not for a few months; most of the change comes in the first year, and the voice can keep settling for two years or more. While it changes, voice breaks, a scratchy or tired voice, a wobbly or smaller range and an achy throat are common. Partly that's your voice learning new controls, so go gently on those days.

**Practice can wait, or come along.** Testosterone changes the vocal folds, not how you use them: the voice you're used to can stay as a habit, and resonance, melody and loudness are still up to you. Some people practise alongside testosterone, some wait to see how their voice settles, and some don't take testosterone and change their voice through practice alone. Any of these is fine. In studies, most people on testosterone were happy with their voice after a year, while a smaller group wanted more change or had a tired, unsteady voice; some of them worked with a voice therapist.

**Record the same reading each month.** Read the check-in below the same easy way each time, and hum down to the lowest note that still feels easy. A month apart, the takes show what changed, with or without testosterone. You don't have to listen back until you want to.

**Breathe low and slow.** A deeper voice rests on easy breath. Sit or stand tall and loose, let your belly move as you breathe in, and pause for a new breath before you run short. If you wear a binder, notice whether it makes these breaths harder; practise at a time when you're not wearing it if you can, and stop whenever breathing feels tight.

**Never push down.** A lower voice should feel easy. Don't force your pitch to the bottom, squeeze your throat, press on your neck or try to hold your voice box down. A tight neck or jaw, an ache or "lump" in your throat, wanting to clear it, or a voice that's rough or gone by the evening all mean: stop, sip some water and rest. Come back another day with a hum.

**Where to begin.** Pitch is the natural first stop, but go in any order. Testosterone changes the space your voice sounds in only a little, so resonance is worth its own practice either way. One small change at a time, then a real phrase.

## monthly-read

The kettle clicked off, and I sat by the window with my tea while the street slowly woke up below.

> Your check-in: read it in your everyday voice, the same easy way each time. Once a month is enough to hear testosterone or practice at work; every visit is fine too.

## low-hum

mmm ↘

> Hum from a comfortable note down to the lowest one that still feels easy, and stop there. It shows where your range reaches this month; it isn't where you'll speak. Stop before it crackles or fades.

## easy-breath

hhh… one, two, three.

> A slow breath in that lets your belly move, a quiet sigh out, then count on the next easy breath. Pause for air before you run short.

## everyday

Hi, it's me. Have you got a minute?
