Resonance is the size of the space your voice sounds in: the tube from your vocal folds up through your throat and mouth to your lips. A bigger space gives a darker, deeper sound; a smaller, tighter one gives a brighter, buzzier sound. Testosterone thickens the vocal folds but changes this space only a little, so a darker resonance is something you can practise with or without it. Tension tends to make the space smaller, which is why darker mostly starts with letting go.

**Hear the difference.** Pant like a big dog, slow and deep: "hhuh, hhuh". Then like a tiny dog, quick and light: "hhih, hhih". The big dog's space is the one you're after. Go back to the big dog and notice how open and unhurried it feels.

**Open the space without pushing.** Drop your jaw as if about to bite into a big apple, then breathe out silently, as if fogging up a mirror. Warm air on your palm and a stretchy, open feeling in your throat mean the space is open. A small "surprised" face can open it a touch more.

**Hum and chew.** Hum on one easy note with your lips closed and chew slowly, as if on a toffee. Then let the start of a yawn open the space while you keep humming. The pitch stays put; only the space changes. That change is resonance.

**Yawn into words.** Begin a gentle yawn-sigh, and on the tail of it say "Oh, hello". Keep the open, roomy feel as the words start. Words with "o", "aw" and "oo" in them are the easiest to keep dark.

**Feel it lower down.** Hum low and easy with a hand flat on your chest, and tap lightly with your fingertips. A buzz in your chest is a clue that your voice is low and easy; it isn't resonance itself, and you don't need to feel it.

**Separate it from pitch.** Hold one easy note and make only the space bigger, then your usual size again. Record the same line twice, once as usual and once with the bigger space, then open **Detailed comparison** under the newer take: it plays them side by side and gives a rough read of whether the newer one is darker or brighter. While you record in this chapter, a darker–brighter strip under the pitch line shows whether the sound is darker or brighter than when the take began: on one held note, the pitch line stays put while the strip moves. Both are rough, and a different mic or distance moves them, so trust your ears first.

**Keep it gentle.** Bigger is not pushed. Don't press your voice box down, tuck your chin, or hold a yawn open. If your neck, jaw or tongue tightens, or your voice turns muffled, swallowed or hooty, make the change smaller and shake out your jaw. Speech therapists sometimes use hands-on techniques around the throat; those are for them to do, not for pressing on your own neck.

## big-dog

hhuh · hhuh · wuh

> A big dog's slow pant, then a lazy "wuh" in the same open space. Then a tiny dog's "hhih" to hear the contrast, and back to the big dog.

## apple-breath

hhhhhh (silent)

> Jaw dropped as if biting a big apple, breathe out without a sound, like fogging a mirror. Notice the warm air and the open throat. There's no pitch for Lilt to track here; it's a feel to keep.

## hum-chew

mmmmmmm

> Hum on one easy note and chew slowly with your lips closed. Then let a yawn begin while you hum. Listen for the sound growing rounder and darker while the note stays the same.

## yawn-hello

haaah… oh, hello.

> A gentle yawn-sigh, then "oh, hello" on the tail of it, keeping the roomy feel. If the yawn makes you tense, use a smaller one.

## chest-hum

mmmmmm

> Hum low and easy with a hand on your chest and tap lightly. A buzz there is a clue to an easy, low voice, not a pass mark. Stop if it takes effort.

## dark-slide

aaah · aaah · aaah

> One easy note, three ahs: your usual space, a bigger one, then your usual again. Keep the pitch and loudness the same and change only the space. Can you hear the difference without looking?

## ah

aaaaaa

> Your check-in: an easy "ah" as in "father", about four seconds, in the biggest space that stays easy today. Next time, play it beside this one, at a similar pitch and loudness so the comparison is fair.

## oh

oooh

> An easy "oh" as in "go", about four seconds. Round your lips a little and keep the space behind them open. Compare only with your earlier "oh".

## dark-words

go · warm · hold · slow · around

> Open the space on each vowel. These are the easy ones to keep dark; "ee" words are harder, so start here.

## dark-hello

Hey, good to see you. How've you been?

> Yawn-sigh first, then the greeting in the same open space. Compare with an ordinary take. The vowels change as you talk; the roomy space behind them stays.

## note-and-space

mmm… Morning. How are you doing?

> Two cues, one short line: hum your spot from the pitch chapter, then keep the big space as the words start. If one slips, go back to just one.
