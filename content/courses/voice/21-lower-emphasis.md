How your speech moves matters as much as where your voice sits. Testosterone doesn't change it: melody, loudness and pace stay up to you. To lean on an important word, many speakers go higher. Leaning on it with a little more loudness, a slightly longer word or a short pause instead, with fewer and smaller jumps up, tends to sound less feminine to listeners. That's a tendency, not a rule: your expression is yours, and a flat, monotone voice isn't the aim.

**Lean with loudness and time.** Pick the important word and give it a little more voice, a little more length, or a tiny pause before it, without lifting your pitch much. "I didn't say he _took_ it" works with any of the three.

**Smaller steps, still moving.** Let your pitch move in smaller steps rather than big swoops. It should still go up and down; a voice held on one line can sound bored or tired.

**Let statements land.** Many statements end with a gentle fall, and that's fine: it can sound settled and sure. Some questions fall too ("Where are we meeting?"). Let the meaning choose; no ending is wrong.

**Find your pace.** An unhurried pace with pauses between ideas leaves room to breathe and keeps your other cues in place. Not slow, just not racing.

**Be heard without pushing.** Getting closer, facing the person and waiting for a gap in the noise do more than extra volume. Keep your breath steady and your consonants crisp, and never shout over noise: if your throat tightens or tires, stop and rest.

**Practise your moments.** Notice when your voice jumps up: on the phone, when you're nervous, excited or laughing. Pick a line you actually say in that moment and practise it here, so it's ready.

**Listen back.** Does it sound like what you meant? On the pitch line, look for movement in smaller steps rather than a flat ruler. Then ask: did it feel easy, and does it sound like you?

## long-way-home

I took the long way home last night, past the river and the old station, and I didn't see a single car.

> Your check-in: read it like you're telling a friend. Lean on a few words with a little more voice or time rather than height.

## took-borrowed

I didn't say he took it. I said he borrowed it.

> Lean on "took" and "borrowed" with loudness and length, keeping your pitch fairly steady. Then try it with a pause before each instead. Which feels more like you?

## weight-not-height

That was the best meal I've had all year.

> Say it once leaning with pitch, going up on "best". Then again leaning with loudness and a longer "best". Listen to how differently they come across.

## landing

I'll be there at six. See you then.

> Let both sentences land with a gentle fall at the end. Settled, not heavy, and no creak needed.

## falling-question

Where are we meeting tomorrow?

> Ask it with a falling ending, then with a rising one. Both are real questions; notice which you'd actually say.

## phone

Hi, I'm calling about my appointment on Thursday.

> A phone line, where voices often jump up. Start near your spot, lean on "appointment" and "Thursday" with time rather than height.

## coffee

Could I get a large coffee, please? Thanks.

> An everyday order: unhurried, a little pause before "large", and let "Thanks" land.

## say-again

Sorry, could you say that again?

> Picture a busy café. Lean in and face them, keep your breath steady and the consonants crisp, at a comfortable volume. Never push to be heard.

## excited-news

Guess what? I got the job!

> Excited news, where pitch likes to fly up. Let it move, and carry some of the excitement in loudness and pace instead of only height.
