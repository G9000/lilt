Hi, friend. Five minutes of setup and a few words in your everyday voice, and you're ready. If you found your starting voice on Home, that count is already your Day 1.

**Set up your corner.** A quiet room, the mic about 20–30 cm from your mouth, and the same setup every time so your takes compare fairly. On a Mac, set Mic Mode to Standard in Control Center if you see it; Voice Isolation changes quiet sounds. The fan is not your duet partner.

**Check your level.** Count to ten below and play it back. You want it clear, with no loud warning. A gappy pitch line on its own doesn't mean you're too quiet: unvoiced sounds like s and h never draw a line.

**Make a take.** Select the counting text, press Record, and allow microphone access if asked. Say the line, press Stop, and wait for it to finish saving. Play it back when you are ready. If there is no sound, check that your microphone is allowed and selected in your browser or app settings, then try a short count again. Saved takes are in [Recordings](#/history).

**Choose your goal.** This guide focuses on a lighter, brighter voice, but what that sounds like is yours to choose. Write a goal in [Practice settings](#/studio/settings); it becomes the note on your wall. Name a situation and a quality you want, such as an easy, light voice for everyday conversation. A flexible range, or simply more ease, are good goals too. A band on a pitch chart cannot define any of these: pitch is one part, alongside resonance, weight and melody.

**Going lower, or not sure yet?** Choose **Which way** in [Practice settings](#/studio/settings): higher, lower or still open. Lilt's tools follow it: your range, the pitch scale, your takes and notes work in any direction, and Lilt only suggests moving your range the way you're going. Going lower has its own part, [Going lower](#/studio/going-lower): a short start on breath, care and what testosterone does to the voice, then pitch, resonance, vocal weight and emphasis for a deeper, darker, fuller voice. Once Which way is Lower, Today's cards come from those chapters in place of the lighter ones, and the cards you've already practised wait where you left them. Warm-ups, and the texts in Bring it together and the Extras, work in any direction: where a hint names a lighter cue, keep your own practised change instead. Pitch, Resonance, Vocal weight, Articulation, Intonation, Laughs and little sounds and Being heard aim lighter; going lower, use the Going lower chapters in their place. Never push your pitch down or squeeze your throat to sound deeper: a lower voice should still feel easy, and if it feels pressed, rough or tired, stop.

**Record your "before".** Read the texts below in your everyday voice. Don't change anything yet. Future you will want this, trust me. You don't have to listen back to it until you want to.

**How every chapter works.** The same small loop each time: watch the chapter's demonstration if it has one, or replay a take you like, try one change, record, listen back, then use it in a real phrase straight away. Five to ten minutes makes a good visit, and short and often beats long and rare.

**Choose just one next step.** After your starting recording, try [hum to hello](#/studio/warmup/hum-to-hello). You do not need to finish every text here first. Next visit, choose one skill and one listening question: higher or lower for pitch, brighter or darker for resonance, lighter or heavier for weight. If a cue is unclear, revisit its demonstration or use a familiar sound; you do not need to make the change bigger.

**Your ears decide.** Lilt shows your pitch and a rough read of your resonance, but numbers describe a take; they don't grade it. The questions that matter: did it feel easy, could I do it again, and do I like how it sounds? Inside your head your voice sounds lower and fuller than it does to anyone else; the recording is closer to what others hear. Listen back on headphones, not a laptop speaker.

**Hard days.** Hearing your own recordings can sting, especially at first. You can rate a card by how it felt without listening back, and the Course guide on [All chapters](#/studio/chapters) has people to talk to.

**Be gentle with your voice.** It should never hurt: stop and rest if it strains. [Looking after your voice](#/studio/your-voice), after Warm-ups, has it all in one place, whichever way you're going: rest, water, warm-ups, how much is enough, the signs to stop and when to see a doctor. When to see a doctor is also at the end of every chapter.

## today

Hello, this is my voice today.

> Your check-in, every visit: say it however your voice wants to today. Listen back for one thing you like.

## level

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten.

> A level check. Play it back: is it clear, with no crackle or loud warning? If it's hard to hear, move a little closer before turning the input up.

## baseline-read

I'm starting something new today, and I'm going to be patient with myself while I learn.

> Your "before": your everyday voice, no changes. These exact words come back later so you can hear how far you've come.

## baseline-talk

What are you hoping your voice will feel like?

> Talk for up to half a minute. Name one place you'd like your voice to feel more like you: a phone call, a café, with friends.
