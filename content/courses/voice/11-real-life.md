Bring a familiar phrase into an everyday moment that you choose. Phone calls can wait until you want to try them.

**Listen first.** Replay a rehearsal you like. Choose one cue and one line that would be useful today, rather than a whole conversation to perfect.

**Rehearse here.** Say the line once in your everyday voice, then with the change you want to carry over. Choose a comfortable version before trying it with someone else.

**Take a small step.** A voice note to yourself, a greeting with someone you trust or a short order can be enough. Choose the situation and pace that feel right for you; you can stop or return to an easier step.

**Come back after.** Record your own line again, or briefly describe how it felt. Ask whether speaking was comfortable and whether the voice felt like yours. Someone else's reaction does not have to be the only measure of the attempt.

**Ready to expand?** The phrase feels easy to begin and repeat in one familiar situation. Try a little more variety when you want to, keeping pauses and ordinary melody.

## answer-phone

Hello, Alex speaking. How can I help?

> Your check-in: use your own name. Listen to whether the opening feels as easy as the rest, without forcing a light or high start.

## flat-white

Hi! Could I get an oat flat white, please? Small is fine.

> Rehearse a real order of your own if you prefer. Use one familiar cue and a conversational delivery.

## appointment

I'd like to book an appointment for Thursday afternoon, if you have one.

> Say it as a useful request. Clear, comfortable speech and getting your message across matter more than holding an exact pitch.

## station

Sorry, could you tell me how to get to the station from here?

> Ask for information in the way that feels natural to you. A question can have different endings; there is no required upward lift.

## call-friend

Hey! I just wanted to hear your voice. Have you got a minute to chat?

> Rehearse the opening first. An actual call is optional; you choose when and with whom to try it.

## lovely-day

Thank you so much, you've been really helpful. Have a lovely day!

> Let the thanks and goodbye sound natural to you. Listen for a comfortable finish, allowing the pitch to fall.
