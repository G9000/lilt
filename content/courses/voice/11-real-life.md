Phone, counter, strangers. The phone is the final boss: they only have your voice.

**Rehearse here.** Then try it for real somewhere small and safe.

**Come back after.** Record the line again while it's fresh.

**Signs it's working.** The first word sounds as practised as the rest. Every try counts.

## answer-phone

Hello, Alex speaking. How can I help?

> Your check-in: answering the phone. Use your own name instead of Alex, and keep the "hello" as light as the rest.

## flat-white

Hi! Could I get an oat flat white, please? Small is fine.

> Friendly and unhurried, the way you'd say it at the counter.

## appointment

I'd like to book an appointment for Thursday afternoon, if you have one.

> Polite and clear, like talking to a receptionist.

## station

Sorry, could you tell me how to get to the station from here?

> A real question: let it lift a little at the end.

## call-friend

Hey! I just wanted to hear your voice. Have you got a minute to chat?

> Like calling someone close. Yes, an actual phone call. Millennials, we believe in you.

## lovely-day

Thank you so much, you've been really helpful. Have a lovely day!

> Bright and easy all the way to the last word.
