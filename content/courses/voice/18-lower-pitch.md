Pitch is how high or low your voice sounds: how fast your vocal folds vibrate. A lower pitch is one part of a deeper voice, alongside resonance, weight and how your speech moves. There's no single number to reach. Look for a spot a little above the bottom of your range, where your voice still moves easily and stays clear, rather than the lowest note you can make.

**Find your floor, gently.** Sigh or hum from somewhere comfortable down to the lowest note that still feels easy, and stop there. Don't chase the note below it. Your speaking voice will live a little above this floor.

**Find an easy spot.** Agree with someone in a relaxed, lazy "uh-huh", as if you're only half listening. It tends to land somewhere easy and fairly low. Hum on that note, then say a few numbers from it.

**If you take testosterone.** Your floor moves down over the months, and it's common to keep speaking at your old spot out of habit. Hum down to your floor every few weeks, and when a lower spot feels easy in words, let your speaking voice follow it there. If your voice cracks or wobbles while it changes, keep things small and easy on those days.

**Lower is never pressed.** If the sound turns rough, rattly or creaky, or your throat tightens, you've gone lower than your voice wants today: come up a little. A creak isn't a lower pitch, and forcing your pitch down is one of the quickest ways to tire your voice. You can learn other, comfortable ways to sound deeper in the next chapters.

**Anchor it.** Hum your spot, then open the hum straight into counting. The hum gives you the note; the counting turns it into speech.

**Let it move.** Speech isn't one held note. Start near your spot and let your pitch rise and fall with what you mean. [Emphasis and melody](#/studio/lower-emphasis) shows how to keep that movement without big jumps up.

**Listen back.** The pitch scale after each take describes where it sat; it isn't a target. Once you know your spot, save the range around it as your [comfortable range](#/studio/settings), so the recorder shows it behind your pitch line. With Which way set to Lower, Lilt only offers to move that range down.

## low-sigh

haaah ↘

> A slow, easy sigh from the middle of your voice downward, like settling into a chair. Let it fade out before the bottom; don't push the last bit.

## uh-huh

uh-huh · uh-huh · mm-hm

> Relaxed agreement, as if you're only half listening. Notice where it lands: that's often a comfortable speaking spot.

## floor-hum

mmm ↘ … mmm

> Hum down to the lowest note that's still easy, then come back up a few notes and hold a hum there. The second hum is nearer to where you'll speak.

## hum-into-count

mmm… one, two, three, four, five.

> Hum your spot, then open it into counting without a gap. Keep the numbers sounding like speech, not a chant.

## low-count

One, two, three, four, five.

> Your check-in: hum somewhere easy (your spot, once you've found it), then count. Let the numbers move a little, like normal speech.

## low-hello

Hey, how's it going?

> Start near your spot and let the greeting go where it wants. If the end slides up, that's fine; you don't need to hold it down.

## low-phrase

Yeah, that works for me. See you later.

> An everyday line from your spot. If your voice drops into a creak at the end of a phrase, take a fresh breath before the next one rather than pushing through.

## low-count-20

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty.

> A longer run. Voices tend to drift or tire as they go; take a breath and hum your spot again whenever you need to.
