Short things you actually say, in a voice that feels like you. You can borrow one of these from your very first warm-up visit.

**Listen first.** Replay an easy take of a greeting you liked. Notice one quality you want to keep; there is no need to copy somebody else's whole delivery.

**Try one thing.** Record a familiar version, then a version with your chosen small change. Use your own name, order or wording when it makes the phrase more useful.

**Use it today.** Choose one line for a low-pressure moment: greeting someone, asking for something or making a voice note to yourself. Rehearsing here is enough if you don't want to use it with another person yet.

**Listen back.** Which version do you prefer, and can you say it comfortably again? Natural for you is the goal; higher, brighter or more expressive is not a rule for every sentence.

**Ready for more?** You can start the phrase without a long preparation and still feel comfortable. Longer reading and conversation can grow from that.

## coffee

Hi, could I get a coffee, please?

> Your check-in: say it as an ordinary order. Compare two comfortable takes and choose the delivery you prefer.

## black-coffee

Black coffee, please.

> Let the meaning guide the melody, including a falling ending if that feels natural.

## free-later

Hi, it's me! Are you free later?

> Picture someone you know. Listen for the one familiar quality you chose, rather than performing every feature at once.

## good-morning

Good morning. How are you today?

> Use it as a real greeting. Allow the question to sound natural in your own speech style.

## thank-you

Thank you so much, that's lovely.

> Choose a warm delivery you like, without forcing a higher ending.

## intro

Hi, I'm Alex. It's really nice to meet you.

> Use your own name. Compare how the greeting begins with the rest of the sentence, then repeat the easier take.
