Talking freely is the hardest step, because your brain is busy with what to say as well as how. Expect your voice to wander here. That's normal, and it's exactly what this chapter practises.

**Think, then speak.** Take a second to decide your first sentence before you press record. Starting well makes the rest easier.

**Short answers first.** Fifteen to thirty seconds is plenty to begin with. Build towards a minute over the weeks.

**Pause instead of pushing.** When you notice your everyday voice coming back, stop, take a breath, and start the next sentence fresh. Pauses sound natural in conversation; nobody minds them.

**Use it today.** Send someone a voice note, or answer a question out loud while you're on your own.

**Listen back.** Find one moment you liked and one where you drifted. What were you thinking about when it drifted? That's often the clue.

## day

Tell me about your day so far.

> Your check-in: start with fifteen to thirty seconds. Listen for one moment you liked.

## q2

Describe the room you're in right now.

> Name two or three things you can see. Easy content leaves room for the voice.

## q3

What's something you're looking forward to this week?

> Talk about it like you're excited. Notice what happens to your melody when you are.

## q4

Tell me about a place you love to go.

> Up to a minute. Afterwards, name one phrase you liked the sound of.

## q5

What would you cook for a friend tonight, and why?

> Explain it as if they're in the kitchen with you. Pause whenever you need to think.

## q6

What's a small thing that made you smile recently?

> Answer naturally. If your practice voice slips mid-story, pause and pick it back up.
