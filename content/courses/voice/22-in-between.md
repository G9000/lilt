Hi, friend. This part is for a voice somewhere in between: androgynous, nonbinary, neither, both, or simply not decided yet. It has three chapters: this one, for finding a spot in the middle that feels like yours; [Mixing the dials](#/studio/blend), for setting pitch, resonance, weight and melody each where you like them; and [Moving on purpose](#/studio/flex), for going lighter or fuller when you choose and coming back. With Which way set to Still open in [Practice settings](#/studio/settings), they're your Today's cards in place of the chapters aiming one way; open them whenever you like too. Warm-ups, and the texts in Bring it together and the Extras, work alongside them.

**Your goal is yours.** Nobody gets to say what an in-between voice should sound like, and there's no single one to copy. Some people want a voice that listeners can't easily place; some want one that feels right whatever anyone hears; some want to move between a lighter and a fuller voice depending on where they are. Goals often start fuzzy and get clearer as you play, and it's fine for them to change. Write yours in [Practice settings](#/studio/settings) when you have words for it, even rough ones.

**Tell Lilt your direction.** Set **Which way** to Still open. Lilt then won't nudge your range up or down; it offers to widen it either way instead.

**Listen to voices you like.** Now and then, listen to people whose voices you enjoy: friends, presenters, podcasters, characters. What words would you use for them? Notice how one person's voice changes between a phone call, a shop and talking to a pet. That variety is a clue: voices aren't one fixed setting.

**Find your middle.** Hum a gentle siren up and down, only as far as stays easy, and notice the stretch in the middle where the sound is comfortable and clear. Hum there, then say "mm-hm" from it. That's a spot to start from, not a line to stay on.

**The overlap is a guide, not a target.** Many people's speaking voices sit in a stretch where the usual ranges of men and women overlap. The pitch scale after each take marks one such band, 145–165 Hz, and ASHA mentions a slightly wider one, 145–175 Hz. Neither is a goal. Listeners hear more than pitch: in one study, nonbinary speakers were often heard somewhere between men and women, and resonance, weight and melody did as much as pitch to put them there. A middle note with your usual everything else may not sound in between; the next chapter mixes the other dials.

**Never squeeze toward either end.** Your middle should feel as easy as your everyday voice. Don't press your pitch down or strain it up to reach a number. A tight neck or jaw, an ache in your throat, wanting to clear it, or a voice that's tired by the evening all mean: stop, sip some water, and come back another day with a hum.

**Your ears decide.** Lilt's numbers describe a take; they don't say whether you've arrived. In a small study of nonbinary people in voice training, how they felt about their voice didn't always follow how much the numbers moved. Ask: did it feel easy, could I do it again, and does it sound like me?

## this-is-me

Hello, it's me. This is the voice I'm finding, and I'm taking my time with it.

> Your check-in: read it in whatever voice feels most like you today. The same words every visit, so you can hear how it changes.

## both-ways

mmm ↗ ↘

> A slow hum siren up and down, only as far as stays easy. Notice where the sound feels most comfortable and clear: that's often your middle.

## middle-hum

mmm… mm-hm

> Hum somewhere in that comfortable middle, then say "mm-hm" from the same spot, like agreeing with a friend.

## three-spots

hello · hello · hello

> Say it a little lower, then from your middle, then a little higher. All three are allowed. Which feels most like you today?

## middle-count

mmm… one, two, three, four, five.

> Hum your middle, then open it into counting without a gap. Let the numbers move a little, like speech.

## middle-hello

Hey, good to see you. How's your week been?

> Start from your middle and let the greeting go where it wants. It doesn't need to hold one note.
