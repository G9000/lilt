Laughs, sighs, "um"s and "ow!"s happen before you can think about them, which is why they're often the last thing to change. If yours don't match the voice you're building, a little practice helps them catch up. Keep any that already feel like you.

**Try a lighter laugh.** If you want your laugh to match a lighter voice, try one that lives in your face and the front of your mouth instead of your chest. It shouldn't feel forced; think of something actually funny. A big laugh is fine too, if it's yours.

**Fill the pauses.** "Um", "hmm" and "uh" sit right on top of your voice. Practise them on your note so they stop pulling you back to the old one.

**Small reactions.** "Aww", "ow!", "oops" and a sigh are tiny, fast and emotional. Say them as if they're real, then listen to whether they match the rest of your speech.

**Don't clear your throat.** Clearing hard is rough on your vocal folds. Swallow or take a sip of water instead. Coughs and sneezes are fine as they are; there's nothing to practise there.

**Listen back.** Do the little sounds sound like the same person as your sentences? If one jumps out, practise just that one a few times.

## laugh

Haha, no way! That's so funny.

> Your check-in: think of something that actually made you laugh. Keep the laugh light and in the front of your mouth.

## fillers

Um… let me think. Hmm, maybe Thursday?

> Let the "um" and "hmm" sit on your note, so the sentence after them starts in the right place.

## aww

Aww, that's so sweet!

> A soft, warm "aww" that glides down. Let it carry straight into the words.

## oops

Ow! Oops, sorry, I'm fine.

> Quick and real, like you just bumped the table. Is your voice there on the very first sound?

## sigh

Haah… what a long day.

> A tired, friendly sigh, then the words. Keep the sigh easy, not pressed.
