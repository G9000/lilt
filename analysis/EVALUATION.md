# Lilt analysis helper: evaluation (synthetic fixtures only)

_Generated by `scripts/evaluate.py`; do not edit by hand. Re-run after any change to the algorithm, parameters, Praat/Parselmouth version or decoding._

## Status: descriptive only

**Natural-speech validation has NOT been done.** No natural-speech recordings with independently checked reference pitch contours or formant tracks were available. Every result below comes from synthetic signals (tones, noise, glides, vowels) or synthetic text-to-speech. These results show that the software behaves as specified on known signals; they do not show that pitch or formant measurements are accurate on real voices, microphones or rooms. All measures are therefore `use_status: descriptive_only` and must not drive corrective feedback or progression.

## Versions and algorithm actually used

- Parselmouth 0.4.7, bundled Praat 6.1.38 (2 January 2021)
- PyAV 19.0.2 (bundled FFmpeg libavformat 63.1.102), NumPy 2.5.4, Python 3.12.10 on Darwin arm64
- Pitch algorithm used: **`ac`** (`To Pitch (ac)`)
- Selection: 'To Pitch (filtered autocorrelation)' not usable in bundled Praat 6.1.38 (PraatError: Command "To Pitch (filtered autocorrelation)" not available for given objects.); using raw autocorrelation. Self-check: 220 Hz calibration tone -> median 220.000 Hz, ceiling 700 Hz
- Parameters (fixed for every take, no gender-based range): `{"time_step": 0.01, "pitch_floor": 60.0, "max_number_of_candidates": 15, "very_accurate": false, "silence_threshold": 0.03, "voicing_threshold": 0.45, "octave_cost": 0.055, "octave_jump_cost": 0.35, "voiced_unvoiced_cost": 0.14, "pitch_ceiling": 700.0}`
- Formants (resonance): `{"praat_command": "To Formant (burg)", "time_step": 0.01, "max_number_of_formants": 5.0, "maximum_formant": 5500.0, "window_length": 0.025, "pre_emphasis_from": 50.0}`, on pitch-voiced frames only, at the pitch frame times; Praat's undefined values skipped
- Octave-jump ratio 1.8 (and 1/1.8); clip threshold |x| >= 0.999; low signal < -50.0 dBFS RMS; minimum voiced material 0.5 s

Filtered autocorrelation (Praat >= 6.4, which Praat recommends for intonation) is **not** available: the newest Parselmouth on PyPI bundles an older Praat. The code probes for it on every start and will switch automatically (after a calibration self-check) if a newer Parselmouth provides it; that path is untested here. Switching algorithms changes results, so historical takes must then be reanalysed for matched comparisons.

## Known failures found by this evaluation

- **Short octave section now tracked.** In `octave_jump_short` (0.3 s at 400 Hz inside 200 Hz) the gross error rate is 0.0 % with 2 suspected jumps reported, since the AC octave cost was raised from Praat's 0.01 to 0.055 (with 0.01 it stayed at ~200 Hz, 13.4 % gross errors). Subharmonics are still possible on other signals: *the absence of suspected octave jumps does not prove the absence of octave errors.* This fixture is reported, not asserted.
- The octave-jump detector flags any abrupt ratio > 1.8 between adjacent voiced frames. It cannot tell a tracking error from a genuine pitch break (both are in `octave_jump`); jumps are reported, never corrected.
- **AAC/MP4 timing offset.** Voiced segments in `mixed_voiced_unvoiced.mp4` are [[0.5147, 1.0247], [1.5147, 2.2147]] vs truth [[0.5, 1.0], [1.5, 2.2]]: shifted later by up to ~25 ms, and the decoded file is slightly longer than the source. This is consistent with AAC encoder priming samples not being trimmed for this fragmented MP4 (cause not verified). Timestamps from MP4 input should be treated as accurate to about +/- 30 ms. Real Safari recordings were not tested.
- Truncation is detected from decoder evidence (demuxer 'File ended prematurely', corrupt packets, a decode error after valid frames, or a declared duration longer than what decoded). A file cut exactly on a block boundary with no declared duration (typical of MediaRecorder WebM) is indistinguishable from a complete short recording.

## Resonance: synthetic vowels with known formants

Each vowel is made at two pitches and two tract sizes: in the second every formant is 12 % higher, as from a shorter vocal tract (+1.96 semitones). Lilt compares two takes of the same text by the mean change of the F2 and F3 medians in semitones; F1 is reported but left out (see below).

**Why the ceiling is fixed at 5500 Hz.** Praat's guidance picks the ceiling per speaker (5000 Hz for a typical man, 5500 Hz for a typical woman). Lilt never chooses settings from a gender, and a voice in training must be measured the same way every time, so one ceiling has to work across tract sizes. Known limits: F1 sits between widely spaced harmonics when pitch is high and is pulled toward them, so it is not used for comparisons. Formants move with every vowel, so a resonance change is only reported between takes of the same text, recorded with the same microphone (the same rules as pitch). Synthetic vowels are steady and noise-free; real speech moves between vowels and consonants, so real numbers will be noisier than these. Across the sweep a tract change was misread by up to 0.96 st at the extremes, so Lilt words a change under 0.5 st as about the same, 0.5–1 st as a little brighter or darker, and only a change over 1 st plainly.

## What is not evaluated

- Natural speech with independent reference contours (the evaluation asset that is missing). Until it exists, measurements stay descriptive only.
- Real browser recordings (Chrome/Firefox WebM, Safari MP4) from real microphones; only PyAV encodes were decoded.
- Noisy, reverberant or low-SNR voiced speech; breathy, creaky or very high voices; voices near the 60 or 700 Hz limits.
- Formants on natural speech, nasal or breathy voices, pitches above 300 Hz, or real microphones and rooms (whose frequency response can tilt F2/F3).
- Any link between these measures and perceived femininity or lightness. None is claimed: a resonance comparison only says whether F2 and F3 moved up or down between two takes of the same text.
